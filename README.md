# Fiodor

> *"The dungeon doesn't care about your story. That's why it's the only place you can tell it honestly."*

A dialogue-based RPG in the vein of Disco Elysium. You play Fiodor — not a hero, not a chosen one — a dungeon delver the world has largely stopped noticing. The game takes place in the dark, inside encounters, and inside his head.

---

## 1. Overview / Premise

Fiodor is a dungeon delver in the way that an old knife is a kitchen tool: functional, worn, kept around because nothing has replaced it yet. He is not an adventurer in the romantic sense. He did not answer a call. He descended because there was nothing above ground worth staying for, and he kept descending because the dungeon, unlike the surface, at least has consistent rules.

The world confirmed long ago that Fiodor is nobody. Low birth, failed apprenticeship, a reputation assembled from other people's contempt. The dungeon did not confirm this. The dungeon does not care about station. The dungeon made him into something — something patient, dangerous, attentive in the way that only fear can make a person — and that something does not map cleanly onto the nobody the world decided he was.

This is the tension the game lives in.

**Station** is the social self: the man who knows exactly how he is perceived, who carries every slight like a stone in a pack he can't put down. **Instinct** is what grew in Station's place underground — the part of Fiodor that has started to listen to the dark rather than fear it. Between them, **Spite** and **The Deep** mark the poles: the furious human animal who will not lie down, and whatever vast, geological thing is slowly replacing him.

Every encounter is a choice between these versions of himself. The dungeon is the setting. Fiodor's interiority is the subject.

---

## 2. The Four Attributes & Skills

Skills are not numbers on a sheet. They are voices — distinct, opinionated, occasionally at odds with each other and with Fiodor's better judgment. When a skill fires, it speaks. When a check fails, it has something to say about that too. The skill system is a cast of internal characters.

---

### FLESH

Your body as a ledger of damage taken and survived. You are not strong in the heroic sense — you are durable in the way a broken thing that didn't break all the way is durable.

| Skill | Voice | Sample Interjection |
|---|---|---|
| Endurance | Slow, stubborn, almost bovine. Doesn't argue — just states what the body will and won't do. Occasionally quietly proud. | *Three. You've handled three. Don't think about three. Think about the first one.* |
| Scarring | Bitter and specific. Every wound remembers the thing that made it. Interjects with dangerous knowledge earned the worst way. | *The hesitation is what gets you killed. You learned that. Remember where you learned that.* |
| Hunger | Feral, impatient, embarrassing. Notices food, warmth, rest constantly. Sharpens to a fine point when survival is on the line. | *That smells edible actually. To you, I mean. Not just them.* |

---

### WIT

The mind that got smart because it had to, not because it wanted to. Not academic intelligence — survival intelligence. Pattern recognition in the dark. Knowing what something is worth before you've decided whether to take it.

| Skill | Voice | Sample Interjection |
|---|---|---|
| Dungeon Lore | Pedantic and paranoid in equal measure. Catalogues monsters, traps, and architecture obsessively. Deeply distrusts anything it hasn't seen before. | *Limestone this wet means an active water table nearby. The iron smell is oxidised mineral deposits or old blood. Statistically, mineral deposits.* |
| Appraisal | Mercenary, unsentimental. Everything has a price. Interjects to value loot, assess threats as costs, calculate whether this situation is worth it. | *You assessed the situation and missed the real cost. What were you actually paying attention to?* |
| Wayfinding | Quiet and spatial. Reads rooms and tunnels like text. Occasionally philosophical about passages as metaphor. Gets uneasy in circular logic and circular corridors equally. | *The geometry hasn't changed. The passage is the same width it was. Your perception of it has changed. Note the difference.* |
| Scavenging | Sees potential where others see waste. Not just finding things — understanding how things can become other things. | *Someone died here with their pack still on. Whatever they brought wasn't enough for them. Might be enough for you — you're not fighting the same fight they were.* |

---

### STATION

Your place in the world — which is to say, near the bottom of it. You have been looked past, looked down on, or looked at like a problem. Station is about the social grammar of being nobody.

| Skill | Voice | Sample Interjection |
|---|---|---|
| Reputation | Wounded pride with a long memory. Knows exactly what people say about you and why. Swings between wanting to prove them wrong and suspecting they're right. | *Three goblins. Three. This is going in the ledger.* |
| Deception | Weary and practical. The social tools of the powerless — misdirection, playing small, telling people what they want. Not malicious, just experienced. | *You told yourself you were turning back for a good reason. Were you.* |
| Spite | Hot, energetic, motivating. The chip on the shoulder as a fuel source. Talks you into things you shouldn't attempt. Occasionally gets you through them. Also the voice that tempts Fiodor to become the thing that was done to him. | *You are not dying in a ditch eight feet wide. Get up.* |

---

### INSTINCT

What the dungeon has grown inside you in place of the things it took. Spend enough time underground and something changes. You stop thinking about the dark and start listening to it.

| Skill | Voice | Sample Interjection |
|---|---|---|
| Danger Sense | Wordless and urgent. Interrupts constantly in threatening areas. Doesn't explain itself — just insists. Gets offended when ignored and vindicated when correct. | *I told you. I told you something was wrong and you overrode me. I want that acknowledged.* |
| Superstition | Strange, lyrical, irrational-but-right. Knows the old delver rituals and folk wisdom. Interprets omens. Occasionally correct in ways that can't be explained. | *Bad luck to set the dungeon against itself. It remembers.* |
| The Deep | The oldest voice. Slow, vast, barely verbal. Connects you to whatever is ancient and geological about the dungeon itself. Unsettling. Knows things it shouldn't. | *These ones are smarter than their kind usually runs. Something has been teaching them.* |

---

The tension between Station and Instinct is Fiodor's core psychological conflict. Spite and The Deep are the two poles: the angry human self and whatever he is becoming. Every scenario should have a branch those two can hijack.

## 3. Scenario Design Philosophy

The game does not have combat in the mechanical sense. Every encounter is a dialogue — with the dungeon, with whatever is in it, and with the voices in Fiodor's head. The goblin encounter is the design template.

### How Skills Interact

A worked example: Fiodor enters a room. Three goblins. They haven't seen him yet.

**Sneak past / poison the food.** Danger Sense goes quiet — the good kind of quiet, the kind that means the path is open. Scavenging notes that whatever they're eating could be made worse with the right addition. Hunger is mildly offended that you're wasting it. This approach rewards Instinct investment: Wayfinding maps the room, Danger Sense tracks patrol rhythms, Scavenging improvises the tools. Station stays mostly silent. This is not a moment about what the world thinks of you.

**Ambush / terrify them.** Spite wants this. Spite has wanted this since you spotted them. Scarring knows exactly how to open the engagement — it has a memory for these moments. Reputation watches, tallies, takes notes. The risk: this is also the branch where Spite starts to sound like the people who wronged you. Winning ugly is still winning, but The Deep finds it distasteful, and will say so.

**Trick them.** Deception operates here with the weary competence of someone who has been underestimated their whole life and learned to weaponise it. You play small. You play confused. You give them the version of Fiodor that confirms what they already think — nobody, no threat — and you walk out. The wrinkle: goblins trick back. If they're trying to run the same play on you, Wit has to catch it. Dungeon Lore knows goblin gambits. Appraisal suspects you're being valued poorly on purpose. The Deep goes very still, which is either nothing or everything.

**Lure a larger predator into them.** Wayfinding needs to know the dungeon well enough to make this work. Superstition insists on the ritual: you are making the dungeon complicit, and it should be asked properly. Danger Sense will be anxious throughout — this approach requires staying close to something worse than goblins. The reward is proportional to the risk. The Deep approves. The dungeon feeds itself.

**Lure them into a trap.** Scavenging built the trap; Dungeon Lore designed it; Appraisal costed the materials and decided it was worth it. Deception is the actual lure — something dropped, something implied, a small performance. This is the approach that most resembles patient expertise. Endurance, if the wait is long. Hunger, if the wait is longer.

**Talk your way through.** The broadest branch and the most expensive. Possible approaches:
- *Ally* — offer something. Appraisal sets the price. Deception manages the impression.
- *Threat* — Spite knows how to make this credible. Scarring provides the backing material.
- *Fake goblin* — deeply inadvisable, deeply tempting if Deception is high. Dungeon Lore knows enough to be dangerous. Reputation notes that this will go in the ledger one way or another.
- *Turn them on each other* — Deception and Reputation in tandem. You have to know what they want more than they want you. Wayfinding helps you find the exit while they're busy.

**Other approaches.** Play dead (Endurance carries this; Hunger threatens to undermine it). Bribe them (Appraisal names the number; Reputation notes what it costs to be bought off by goblins). Become their problem (Spite's idea; Deception's execution; everyone else's regret). Wait them out (Endurance, Hunger in escalating conflict, The Deep eventually speaks).

### The Design Logic

Skills are not stat gates. A high Danger Sense doesn't just improve your sneak check — it sulks when you override it, gets vindicated when you're wrong, and shapes how those moments feel. Spite doesn't just fuel aggression — it tempts. It is the voice that says *you could take their things on the way out* when you pass an injured stranger, and it is up to Fiodor to answer it.

The goal is that every approach to an encounter touches at least two or three skills in ways that feel specific rather than mechanical. The interjections should read as texture, pressure, character — not tooltips.

## 4. Systems Reference

### Dialogue Engine

Dialogue is node-based, defined in `dialogueData.ts`. Each node contains:

- **Narrator text** — the scene, delivered in second person
- **Beats** — an ordered list the player advances through one at a time via the `…` continue button. Each beat is either a **voice** (a skill's interjection, tagged with its name and attribute) or a **passive check** at an authored position. Beats are never shown all at once.
- **Player choices** — some always visible, some gated on bonuses

The interface is an infinite-scroll log. History fades upward. The current node is live at the bottom. Speaker portraits appear for skill voices (not the Narrator), colour-coded by attribute: FLESH, WIT, STATION, INSTINCT each have a distinct colour. The Narrator has no portrait.

---

### Skill Checks

**Active checks** are triggered by a player choice. Roll pool × size dice (e.g. 3d8).
- **Fail** if any die shows 1
- **Pass** if all dice avoid 1
- **Stress** if any die shows an odd number and no dice show 1 — pool increases by 1, max 8

The stress spiral is intentional: more dice means more chances to fail and more chances to stress again. Skills become less reliable the harder you lean on them.

**Passive checks** sit at authored positions in a node's beat sequence and are rolled the moment the player advances to them — not pre-rolled on entry. Same dice mechanics, but odd results never cause stress. On a **pass**, the check's tag line and its skill message pop in immediately (and any bonus is granted); on a **fail**, the beat is silently skipped — the player never sees it, Disco Elysium style. Because each passive resolves at its own beat, two passives are never rolled together and revealed out of order.

---

### The Skill Stat Block

Each skill has two values:

| Value | Range | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Size** | d4 / d6 / d8 / d10 / d12 | The face value of each die rolled |
| **Pool** | 1–8 | The number of dice rolled |

Default is 1d4 / pool 1. At character creation, the player allocates upgrades across skills before play begins.

---

### Stress

When a check passes with at least one odd die, the skill stresses. Pool increases by 1 (max 8). The skill row in the sidebar flashes amber.

More dice increases both failure probability and stress probability. This is the system's central tension: competence is fragile. The skills you rely on are the ones that will eventually let you down in the worst way.

---

### The Bonus System

Passive check successes (and future game events) can grant bonuses that persist until consumed. Three types:

| Bonus | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Size step-up** | The skill's die size increases one step (d4→d6, etc.) for one check |
| **Ignore stress** | Roll only 1dX for one check regardless of pool — stress cannot trigger |
| **Unlock choice** | Adds a dialogue option that wasn't previously available |

Bonuses are stored in game state and applied automatically when the relevant check fires. The check log entry records when a bonus was active.

---

### Character Creation

On boot, a creation screen allows allocation of skill upgrades before play begins. All skills start at 1d4 / pool 1. The player assigns:

- 2 skills to **d6**
- 2 skills to **d8**
- 1 skill to **d10**

No skill can be assigned twice. The game does not begin until all five slots are filled. These choices are the first characterisation decision: they tell you, before a word of dialogue, what kind of delver Fiodor has become.

---

### Sound

All sound is synthesised procedurally via the Web Audio API. No audio assets. Each source type has a distinct voice:

| Source | Sound |
|---|---|
| Narrator message | Filtered noise scribble |
| FLESH skills | Low thudding tone |
| WIT skills | Crisp high bell |
| STATION skills | Warm mid-range with vibrato |
| INSTINCT skills | Deep resonant drone |
| Check pass | Ascending two-note figure |
| Check fail | Low thud with noise burst |
| Check stress | Dissonant sawtooth clash |

The sounds are functional feedback, not decoration. Stress is audibly wrong. The Deep sounds like the dungeon. That's the goal.

## 5. Dialogue Data Format

Nodes, beats, and choices are all defined in `dialogueData.ts`. The key types:

### Nodes & Beats

```ts
interface DialogueNode {
  id: string
  narrative: string
  beats: Beat[]            // ordered; revealed one at a time
  choices: DialogueChoice[]
}

type Beat = VoiceBeat | PassiveBeat

interface VoiceBeat {
  kind: 'voice'
  id?: string
  speaker: string
  text: string
}

interface PassiveBeat {
  kind: 'passive'
  id?: string
  skillKey: SkillKey
  successInterjection: Interjection   // shown only on a pass
  successBonus?: { type: BonusType; skillKey?: SkillKey; unlockKey?: string; sourceDescription: string }
}
```

A node's content is one ordered `beats` array. Voice beats reveal directly when reached. Passive beats roll at that moment: on a pass they reveal a `[PASSIVE]` tag plus the `successInterjection` and grant any `successBonus`; on a fail they are silently skipped. Interleave passives among voices to place a check exactly where its message belongs.

### Choices

```ts
interface DialogueChoice {
  id?: string           // stable handle for referring to this link (e.g. "goblin_start/sneak")
  text: string
  nextNodeId: string
  check?: { skillKey: SkillKey; failNodeId?: string }
  requiresUnlock?: string   // hidden until a matching unlock_choice bonus is pending
}
```

`id` is optional but should be set on any choice you want to refer to in conversation. The convention is `node/choice-id` (e.g. `goblin_spotted/bolt`).

`requiresUnlock` gates a choice behind an `unlock_choice` bonus. The choice is invisible until the bonus exists; taking the choice consumes the bonus.

### Interjections

```ts
interface Interjection {
  id?: string    // optional reference handle; fill in when needed
  speaker: string
  text: string
}
```

## 6. The Goblin Chamber (Current Test Scenario)

Three goblins stand between Fiodor and the onward passage. They haven't seen him. The encounter is built in passes — new approaches are added incrementally rather than all at once.

### Node map

```
goblin_start  ── beats: [voice DangerSense] · [passive Wayfinding → +die to DangerSense]
               │         [voice Scarring]    · [passive Scavenging → unlocks /poison]
               │
               ├─ sneak   → Danger Sense ──┬─ pass ──→ goblin_slip
               │                           └─ fail ──→ goblin_spotted
               │
               ├─ poison  → Deception ─────┬─ pass ──→ goblin_poison_success   [unlocked only]
               │            (requires       └─ fail ──→ goblin_poison_caught
               │             corpse-veil)
               │
               └─ observe → goblin_observe ─┬─ sneak  (same Danger Sense check)
                                            └─ poison (same Deception check, still gated)

goblin_spotted       /bolt → Endurance ─┬─ pass → goblin_escaped
goblin_poison_caught /bolt → Endurance ─┘         └─ fail → goblin_cornered
```

### Current passes

| Pass | Approach | Primary check | Support |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sneak past | Danger Sense | Wayfinding passive (+die) |
| 2 | Poison food | Deception | Scavenging passive (unlock) |

Planned: ambush, terrify, lure-monster, lure-trap, ally/threat/fake-goblin/divide talking gambits, last-stand combat.

### Shared failure terminals

`goblin_spotted` and `goblin_poison_caught` both funnel into the same **bolt** → Endurance check → `goblin_escaped` / `goblin_cornered`. Each has its own framing (tin shard underfoot vs. hand in the meat) but the mechanical branch is shared. New approaches that end in discovery will route here too.

`goblin_cornered` is the designated hook for the future combat pass.

## 7. Debug Tools

Three debug tools are available during development. None affect production logic.

### P / F / S — Force check outcome

Press **P**, **F**, or **S** to arm a forced outcome for the next check (pass / fail / stress). The key toggles — press again to cancel. A status label appears bottom-left when a force is armed. The force clears automatically after any check fires (active or passive).

Useful for testing branches that require unlikely dice results: arm **P**, jump to a node, and the passive will resolve however you want.

### `/` — Debug console

Press **`/`** to open a command palette. Currently supported:

| Command | Effect |
|---|---|
| `goto <node-id>` | Jump directly to any node |

The console filters matching node ids as you type, showing each id alongside a snippet of its narrative. **↑ / ↓** selects, **Tab** completes, **Enter** jumps, **Esc** closes.

`goto` clears the target node from the resolved-passive registry, so passive checks re-fire on arrival — every jump is a clean entry.

Composing the two tools: `/goto goblin_start` then press **P** to force the Scavenging passive to pass and reveal the poison choice.
